Finding a low-cost route through a network, estimating the probability of a rare and extreme event, simulating a strongly correlated material: each of these means searching among possibilities whose number grows exponentially with the size of the problem. Tensor networks and certain quantum circuits can find and use the low-dimensional structure hidden inside that growth. I have developed these methods for more than ten years and applied them across quantum physics problems and in industry, in finance, logistics, mobility and energy.

## Why quantum

Five friends each picking one of four burgers already make 1,024 possible orders, and every added choice doubles the count, so for large problems even a supercomputer settles for good guesses. Qubits hold every combination at once, and when the problem is encoded and the machine steered correctly, quantum interference gathers the probability onto the right answer. Where information moves through the machine only a little, tensor networks on the computers we already have can still follow it. I build the methods on both sides of that line (classical vs. quantum) and have often walked the fine line (hybrid classical-quantum) to bring out the best of both worlds.

## Research

- **Strongly correlated fermions.** Fully fermionic simulation codes, and with them pairs that move and junctions of quantum wires.
- **Frustrated magnets.** One model of frustrated spin chains, with frustration as its single dial, for two real crystals.
- **Topological phases in cold atoms and chains.** A test showing that one proposed platform for Majorana qubits loses its protection to a single weak impurity.
- **Information dynamics.** A universal 2/3 rule for the bright fringes at the edge of a quantum light cone, the figure above.
- **Effect of measurements, resets, dissipation and noise.** The entanglement that a record of reset times reveals, tested on superconducting qubits.
- **Tensor networks beyond quantum physics.** Quantum dynamics, random processes and optimisation on the computers we already have.
- **Rare and extreme events.** The odds of a rare event computed directly from the rules of the process.
- **Optimisation on quantum and classical hardware.** Quantum, quantum-inspired and classical solvers benchmarked side by side on problems from industry.
- **Forecasting with quantum systems.** Quantum reservoirs designed in simulation for wind, weather and volatility.
